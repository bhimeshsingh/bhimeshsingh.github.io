Each petition type has its own legal standard. The work is matching a client's record to the right standard, and presenting it so the officer can see it clearly.

Form I-140

EB-1A

Extraordinary ability

A first preference category for individuals who can demonstrate sustained national or international acclaim in their field. It calls for a rigorous, criteria-by-criteria evidentiary case and a persuasive overall narrative.

  • Assessment of the client's record against the regulatory criteria
  • Evidence compilation and recommendation letter drafting
  • Full legal brief and form preparation
Form I-140

EB-2 NIW

National Interest Waiver

A second preference category that lets qualifying professionals self-petition without a job offer or labor certification, where the proposed endeavor has substantial merit and national importance. The strength of the case lies in how clearly the endeavor, and the client's position to advance it, are shown.

  • Defining the proposed endeavor and its national importance
  • Aligning the evidence with each element of the legal standard
  • Anticipating RFE issues at the drafting stage
Form I-129

O-1A

Extraordinary ability, nonimmigrant

A temporary work category for individuals of extraordinary ability in the sciences, education, business or athletics. It suits professionals who need a faster route to working in the United States while building toward long-term options.

  • Petition strategy and evidence mapping
  • Consultations and itinerary documentation
  • Express filings on compressed timelines
Forms I-140 & I-129

EB-1B, EB-1C and L-1

Researchers and corporate transferees

EB-1B serves outstanding professors and researchers, and EB-1C serves multinational executives and managers. L-1A and L-1B petitions cover intracompany transferees who are managers, executives or hold specialized knowledge. Each requires careful structuring of the employer relationship alongside the individual's qualifications.

Post-filing

RFEs, NOIDs and appeals

Representation after filing

When USCIS issues a Request for Evidence or a Notice of Intent to Deny, the response must address the officer's specific concerns with focused evidence and legal argument. I also handle denial reviews and assess motions and appeals, including Form I-290B filings and AAO appeals.

  • Issue analysis and response strategy
  • Supplemental evidence and legal briefing
  • Motion and appeal assessment

The process

Filing typically takes place within one and a half to two months of intake.

Consultation

A prospect call to understand the background, goals and urgency.

Intake

Structured collection of documents, achievements and supporting information.

Case strategy

Selection of the category and an evidence plan aligned to the legal standard.

Drafting

Forms, legal brief, evidence sections and recommendation letters.

Quality review

Legal sufficiency and evidentiary strength reviewed before filing.

Filing and follow-up

Submission, ongoing client updates, and response to any RFE or NOID.
